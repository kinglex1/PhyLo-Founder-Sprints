# FI Set 4 — Pitch Madlibs

## Part 1: Top Ideas to Pitch

Drawing from Set 1 (Ideation) and aligned to Set 3 (Purpose), the three strongest ideas to pitch are:

1. **Month-End Fuel Audit** — The winner from the Ideaster landing page testing. Monthly recurring audit reports for finance directors. Sticky, recurring, clear buyer.
2. **Parked-Truck Alert Sanity Filter** — The original wedge from customer conversations. Solves alert fatigue for ops teams by filtering bogus theft alerts before they land.
3. **Theft Case File Reports** — Forensic incident reports on demand. One-time, high-value, court-ready. Good for proving the model works before selling subscriptions.

---

## Part 2: One-Sentence Pitches (Madlibs Format)

**Pitch 1 — Month-End Fuel Audit:**
> "My company **PhyLo** is developing a **monthly audit report that explains every unexplained diesel loss** to help **fleet finance directors** stop writing off 5–10% of their fuel spend as unproveable theft **with physics-validated cause attribution that no other tool provides**."

**Pitch 2 — Parked-Truck Alert Sanity Filter:**
> "My company **PhyLo** is developing a **smart alert filter that sits above existing telematics** to help **fleet operations teams** eliminate the 90% of parked-truck fuel alerts that are false alarms **with physics modelling that distinguishes real theft from tank slosh and grade effects**."

**Pitch 3 — Theft Case File Reports:**
> "My company **PhyLo** is developing an **on-demand forensic incident report** to help **fleet operators and HR teams** prove whether a fuel-loss event was theft, sensor error, or mechanical issue **with a physics-grounded audit document they can use in disciplinary hearings or court**."

---

## Part 3: LLM Critique and Improvements

**Pitch 1 — Month-End Fuel Audit:**
- *Critique:* The sentence is long and the "secret sauce" (physics-validated cause attribution) is buried. "Unproveable" is spelled wrong.
- *Improvement:* Tightened the secret sauce to lead. Fixed spelling. Split into two stronger sentences.
- *Revised:*
  > "My company **PhyLo** is developing a **monthly fuel audit report** to help **fleet finance directors** replace the 'write it off' reflex with auditable cause attribution — with **physics-validated explanations no other tool can provide.**

**Pitch 2 — Parked-Truck Alert Sanity Filter:**
- *Critique:* "Smart alert filter" is vague and sounds like a feature, not a product. The 90% stat is useful but needs sourcing. "Tank slosh and grade effects" is too technical for a one-liner.
- *Improvement:* Reframed as a product (Sanity Layer, not filter). Made the pain concrete. Simplified the science.
- *Revised:*
  > "My company **PhyLo** is building a **validation layer that filters bogus parked-truck fuel alerts** to help **fleet ops teams** stop wasting time on 50 false alarms a night so they can act fast when real theft happens — using **physics-based rules that tell the difference between a real siphon and a truck on a slope**."

**Pitch 3 — Theft Case File Reports:**
- *Critique:* "On-demand forensic incident report" is solid but the audience could be tighter. "Disciplinary hearings or court" covers a lot of ground. The secret sauce is clear.
- *Improvement:* Narrowed the audience to the decision-maker. Kept the legal defensibility angle as the hook.
- *Revised:*
  > "My company **PhyLo** is building a **forensic fuel incident report** to help **finance directors** get court-admissible proof when fuel disappears — so they can recover losses, act on theft, or exonerate a driver, **with physics-grounded evidence no opposing party can argue against**."

## Part 4: Selected Pitch for Weekly Strategy Presentation

**Selected: Pitch 1 — Month-End Fuel Audit** (refined version)

> "My company **PhyLo** is developing a **monthly fuel audit report** to help **fleet finance directors** replace the 'write it off' reflex with auditable cause attribution — with **physics-validated explanations no other tool can provide.**

**Why this one:**
This is the winning idea from the Ideaster testing, the tightest alignment with the pilot partner's stated need, and the most commercially specific pitch. It names the exact buyer (finance director), the exact pain (monthly write-offs), and the exact gap we fill (physics-validated explanations) in one sentence. It also survives the "so what?" test — the person hearing it immediately asks "what do you mean by physics-validated?" which opens the conversation rather than closing it.

**Pitch 2 (Alert Sanity Layer)** is the better opener for a technical or ops audience and should be kept ready as an alternative.
