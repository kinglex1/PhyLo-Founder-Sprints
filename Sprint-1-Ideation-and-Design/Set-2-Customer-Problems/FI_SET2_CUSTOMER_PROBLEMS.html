Part 2 — Five Customer Problems Synthesized

1

The False Positive Avalanche

HIGH FREQUENCY, HIGH COST

Who has it: Fleet managers running dual-tank vehicles on hilly terrain (N4 Platinum Corridor). Every time a vehicle parks on an incline, fuel shifts between tanks and triggers a theft alert. 30–50 false alerts per week per vehicle. The ops team stops trusting the system entirely.

Why existing tools fail: Rule-based threshold alerts cannot distinguish a 50L siphoning event from a 30L dual-tank fuel shift on a 5° incline.

PhyLo's angle: Physics-informed model accounting for tank geometry, vehicle angle, fuel density shift, and thermal effects — eliminates false positives at their source.

2

The Accountability Gap at Month-End

HIGH SEVERITY

Who has it: Every fleet manager who presents monthly fuel reconciliation to finance. The number is consistently 5–8% higher than it should justify. Finance asks "why?" The manager says "theft and variance." The conversation ends there.

Why existing tools fail: Systems flag anomalies — they do not classify, attribute, or produce audit-grade evidence. A flag is not a case file.

PhyLo's angle: Month-End Fuel Audit report — vehicle by vehicle, route-verified consumption, anomalies classified with confidence scores, alternative hypotheses eliminated, loss values in Rands. Finance-ready, audit-grade, legally defensible.

3

Cross-Border Dwell Time and Organized Theft

HIGH SEVERITY, CONTEXTUAL

Who has it: Fleet operators running SA-to-Zimbabwe, SA-to-Mozambique routes. Vehicles wait 24–72 hours at Beitbridge/Plumtree in open, unsecured areas. Organized siphoning events cause 600L+ losses per incident. Operators receive alerts. Drivers say nothing happened. No proof. No action.

Why existing tools fail: GSM coverage gaps mean telematics drops offline. Extended dwell times create the longest high-risk stationary periods of any route segment. Organized crime networks create demand for stolen fuel (Zimbabwe currency constraints). Driver complicity may be coerced.

PhyLo's angle: CAN bus + odometer fallback during GSM dropout. Border dwell-time attribution. Zone-specific risk scoring. Organized theft pattern detection.

4

Phantom Fills and Ghost Kilometres

MEDIUM FREQUENCY, HIGH FINANCIAL IMPACT

Who has it: Any fleet operating with fuel cards not integrated with tank sensor data. Phantom fill: card records 400L, sensor shows 280L increase — 120L diverted. Ghost kilometres: odometer inflated to mask siphoning by making litres-per-100km appear normal.

The pain: Single phantom fill = R3,900–R6,500. Weekly phantom fill per driver = R202,800–R338,000/year. Driver siphons 50L, inflates odometer 200km — fleet pays twice.

Why existing tools fail: Fuel card data and tank sensor data live in separate silos. Cross-reference never happens. Fraud is invisible.

PhyLo's angle: Fuse card transactions + tank sensors + GPS location at every transaction. If card says 400L but sensor shows 280L — 120L to explain.

5

The Skills Gap: Data Exists, Decisions Don't

SYSTEMIC

Who has it: TMS and fleet management platform operators across South Africa. The primary implementation failure is not technology — it's the skills gap between data collection and data-driven decision making (Technology Innovation in Transport Forum, March 2026).

The pain: A fleet manager cannot extract a monthly fuel audit report from their telematics system without a data analyst's help. Anomalies accumulate in dashboards no one reviews. By the time someone looks, the evidence is degraded, the driver has rotated, and the trail is cold.

PhyLo's angle: Build the monthly review workflow into the product. The Month-End Fuel Audit is a decision-support system, not a report — output a finance director can use and an ops manager can act on, without data analysis skills required.
