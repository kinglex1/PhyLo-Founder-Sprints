The Why Now Shift

Before 2024: Fuel management = GPS + alert thresholds. Systems raise alarms but cannot distinguish theft from mechanical loss, sensor error, or operational variance. Trust score in existing systems: 5/10 average.

Now 2024-2026: AI and physics-based models make audit-grade fuel accountability achievable. Rising diesel prices (ZAR 22/liter) mean 6-10% unexplained losses are no longer tolerable. No competitor offers physics-informed reports that legally qualify as evidence. This is PhyLo's window.

PART 4 — Customer Call Synthesis

Full Interview Synthesis (MD)
8 video calls synthesized. 5+ calls with friends and potential customers, survey administered live, each conversation transcribed and analyzed.

Strongest Signals

1. Pain is real and quantified — Respondents track losses weekly (6-10%), waste hours on false alarms (avg 7.75 hrs/week). This is an active operational crisis, not theoretical concern.

2. Trust deficit is the market opportunity — Current systems score 5/10. Fleet managers are actively looking for something better.

3. Root cause identification is the #1 unmet need — 50% cite root cause identification as biggest frustration. They do not need another alert, they need a diagnosis.

4. Legal defensibility wins enterprise deals — Multiple callers need evidence that holds up in disciplinary proceedings. Audit-grade reports are not a feature, they are the sale.

5. Small fleet market is underserved — Fastest-growing segment (15.70% CAGR), more price-sensitive. Entry-point pricing determines adoption.

Strongest Concerns

Proof before payment — Some want demonstrated results before subscription commit. Freemium or pay-per-incident option needed for trust-building.

Technical constraints — Older diesel vehicles, poor rural connectivity, limited IT infrastructure. PhyLo must design around these.

Zero false positive tolerance — Wrongly accusing drivers destroys trust faster than doing nothing. A 0% false positive rate is not a goal, it is a requirement.
