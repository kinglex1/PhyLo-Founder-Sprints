Business A — Month-End Fuel Audit

Strongest Concerns

⚠ Finance director buyer = longer sales cycle, requires ROI proof before signing

⚠ MICP: operators with telematics infrastructure already — limits addressable segment at launch

⚠ PINN advantage invisible to buyer — they care about the report, not the engine

Business B — Alert Sanity Filter (Dual-Tank False Positive Elimination)

Strongest Signals

✓ Directly reported by pilot partner — "30–50 false alerts a week, we stopped looking at them"

✓ Fastest PINN proof — one week of deployment, false positives gone, trust restored

✓ Genuinely hard to replicate — rule-based competitors cannot solve dual-tank tank physics

✓ TAM: 10,000 dual-tank vehicles × R18k/year = R180M/year

Strongest Concerns

⚠ Risk of being a feature, not a business — operators may expect as add-on to existing telematics

⚠ Value is in elimination — hard to demo "this is what would have happened with the filter"

⚠ Buyer (ops manager) and payer (finance director) are different people — unclear sales motion

⚠ 12–18 month defensibility window before well-funded competitors replicate

Business C — Theft Case File Reports

Strongest Signals

✓ Legal/proof framing resonated with ops managers in testing (4 visits, 0 conversions)

Strongest Concerns

⚠ Legal framing didn't convert in landing page test — too far from daily workflow

⚠ Infrequent purchase — not recurring by default unless bundled into subscription

⚠ Market size smaller — only ~30% of operators actively pursue legal action on fuel incidents

⚠ Path to R1M ARR: Year 3 at earliest — too slow for startup runway

Working Group + Mentor Pressure Test

WG Member 1 — B2B SaaS founder

"The Month-End Fuel Audit is right. My concern: finance directors don't buy SaaS on a handshake. You'll need case studies and ROI calculators before your second customer. Make sure the pilot partner produces a real number you can show."

WG Member 2 — Logistics operator turned founder

"The Alert Sanity Filter is the more compelling story for me. '30–40 false alarms a week' I know that pain. 'Month-End Fuel Audit' sounds like spreadsheets. Consider leading with the filter and upselling the audit."

WG Member 3 — AI/ML technical founder

"The PINN advantage is most defensible in the Alert Sanity Filter — dual-tank physics model is genuinely hard. The audit report is strong but any dev can build a template. The dual-tank model is your real moat."

Mentor 1 — Ex-fleet operator, VC

"I've seen six fuel monitoring startups in Southern Africa die. They sold to the ops manager but ops couldn't sell to finance. Your bet on the finance director is correct — include finance-speak from day one: ROI, payback period, audit-ready reporting."

Mentor 2 — SaaS go-to-market expert

"R50k–R66k/year average deal = mid-market SaaS. That requires a full sales process: demos, trials, procurement, legal. Are you resourced? Consider product-led growth alongside sales-led."

Mentor 3 — PLG specialist

"The mockup and audit demo are your best acquisition tools. Show a fleet ops manager a report that looks like what they wish they had today and they'll share it with finance. The product is the marketing."

Adjustments Made Based on Pressure Test

Adjustment 1 — Lead with the filter in the demo: Set 4 demo opens with false positive problem ("30–50 false alerts a week, solved in one week") before transitioning to the audit report. Filter is the hook; audit is the business.
Adjustment 2 — Deliver a finance-ready number from the pilot: Set 6 (Pilot Partner Validation) includes specific ask: monthly variance in Rands before and after the pilot. That number becomes the first case study for finance director buyers.
Adjustment 3 — Build mockup for sharing: Set 3 mockup includes shareable link and self-serve trial. One-click PDF export of a sample month-end report is the primary demo artifact — not a live system, but a look people want to try.
Adjustment 4 — Separate the sales motion: Ops managers get self-serve trial (mockup + audit report). Finance directors get ROI calculator and case study. Same product, different entry points.

Minimum Viable Customer Profile (MICP)

Who: Fleet operations manager managing 50–100 vehicles with existing telematics infrastructure (GPS + fuel sensor)

Their pain: Monthly fuel reconciliation with 5–8% unexplained variance, no document to give finance, no proof to act on theft alerts

Their budget: R12k–R36k/month for a solution that eliminates the month-end scramble and produces an audit-ready document

Their trigger: Last finance meeting where they had to say "we don't know where R60k went"

Their buyer: Finance director, but needs ops manager champion to evaluate and advocate internally

Where to find them: Gauteng/Rustenburg N4/N1 corridor, cross-border operators (Botswana/Zimbabwe/Mozambique), mining logistics fleets, 50+ vehicle fleets
