## Part 1: Customer Development Script

**Opening (60 seconds)**

> "Thanks for taking the time. I'm Qhayiya from PhyLo — we're building a fuel monitoring system for South African fleet operators. The reason I'm calling is to understand the operational challenges around fuel accountability. There's no pitch today — I just want to learn what you're dealing with. Mind if I record this so I can review my notes later? Everything you share stays anonymous and won't be shared outside the team."

**Block 1 — Context (5 min)**

Goal: Understand their operation, current fuel monitoring setup, and monthly reconciliation process.

1. "Can you tell me about your fleet operation — how many vehicles, what routes, what are you moving?"
2. "Walk me through how fuel accountability works in your operation today. From the moment a driver gets diesel to the moment you reconcile at month-end — what does that process look like?"
3. "What fuel monitoring tools are you using right now? Fuel cards, sensors, GPS, manual processes?"
4. "Who handles the month-end fuel reconciliation? What does finance typically ask for, and what do you have to give them?"

Listen for: Existing infrastructure gaps, who owns the problem, monthly pain frequency.

**Block 2 — Problem Exploration (10 min)**

Goal: Understand the deepest pain points and current solutions.

5. "Walk me through the last time you had to explain a fuel loss to finance. What happened?"
6. "When you get a fuel theft alert on a vehicle — what actually happens next? What do you do with that information?"
7. "Have you ever not taken action on a fuel anomaly because you couldn't prove what happened? What was that situation like?"
8. "Tell me about the false positive situation. How many false alerts do you get per week? What do you do with them?"
9. "What does your best month look like? What does your worst month look like in terms of fuel variance?"

Listen for: Decision paralysis, evidence gaps, false positive volume, variance range.

**Block 3 — Current Solutions and Spending (5 min)**

Goal: Understand WTP and budget authority.

10. "What are you currently paying for fuel monitoring — sensors, software, telematics? What's working, what's not?"
11. "If I could show you a system that produced a month-end report with vehicle-by-vehicle fuel accountability — confidence scores, alternative hypotheses eliminated, loss values in Rands — what would that be worth to your operation?"
12. "Who would write the cheque for a tool like that? You, your finance director, the owner, someone else?"

Listen for: Price sensitivity, decision-making chain, evaluation criteria.

**Block 4 — Decision Process (5 min)**

Goal: Understand buying process, evaluation criteria, concerns.

13. "If you were to evaluate something like this, what would you need to see in a demo or trial to commit to buying?"
14. "Have you ever bought a SaaS product for your fleet operation before? What was that process like?"
15. "What's the biggest reason you'd hesitate to buy something like this — what concerns would you have?"

Listen for: Risk factors, switching costs, technical requirements.

**Block 5 — Cross-Border Specifics (3 min, if relevant)**

Goal: Understand the specific cross-border challenges if applicable.

16. "Do you operate any cross-border routes — Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Botswana?"
17. "How does cross-border travel change the fuel picture for you? What gets harder?"

**Closing (2 min)**

18. "Is there anything I didn't ask that you think I should know about fuel accountability in your operation?"
19. "Is there anyone else in your network managing a similar fleet who would want to have this conversation? Anyone you'd recommend I speak with?"

**Wrap-up**

> "Really appreciate the time. If we end up building something that addresses what you described, I'd love to show it to you in a few weeks. No pressure — just want to keep you in the loop."

## Part 2: Rehearsal Notes

**Tools to use for rehearsal:**

- **Poised or Yoodli** — for practicing delivery, pacing, and clarity
- **AI speaking models** (Grok, ChatGPT voice mode) — to simulate a skeptical customer

**Key things to nail before live calls:**

- **Don't pitch in the first 5 minutes.** Most founders start explaining their solution before the customer has finished describing the problem. Practice asking the question, then shutting up. Wait for the answer.
- **Follow-up probes, not new questions.** When the customer says something interesting, the natural move is a follow-up. "Tell me more about that." "What happened next?" "What was the cost of that?"
- **Don't confirm what they say.** "Right" and "exactly" are conversation-closers. They signal "I'm done with this topic." Use "Tell me more" or silence instead.
- **Don't argue if they say the pain is small.** Some customers won't admit the pain is severe. Probe with: "When you sit down with finance, what does that conversation actually look like?" They'll often admit more when talking about a specific moment.
- **Don't ask about WTP too early.** Get them describing the pain first, the cost second, the WTP third. If you ask "what would you pay?" before they've described the cost of the problem, they'll anchor low.

**Sample AI rehearsal prompt:**

> "I'm a South African fleet operations manager with 60 vehicles, mostly cross-border. We have GPS, fuel sensors, and use fuel cards. I get a lot of false alerts from dual-tank vehicles. I have to explain unexplained fuel losses to finance every month. Interview me about my fuel monitoring challenges. Be skeptical but realistic. Take the conversation in unexpected directions."

## Part 4: Interview Conduct — Process & Tooling

### Tools

- **Transcription:** Otter.ai, Fireflies.ai, or built-in Zoom transcription
- **CRM tracking:** Google Sheet, Notion database, or HubSpot free tier
- **Calendar:** Calendly for self-serve booking (cleaner than back-and-forth emails)
- **Recording:** Zoom (with permission), or phone-recorded (with consent)

### Pre-Interview Checklist

- [ ] Confirm recording permission in writing (email confirmation)
- [ ] Test transcription software
- [ ] Read previous interview notes for this contact (avoid repeating questions)
- [ ] Have the script open but don't read it verbatim
- [ ] Have water, quiet space, working mic

### During Interview

- **First 60 seconds:** Set context, ask for recording permission, give them permission to skip questions
- **Take notes on what they don't say** — gaps in their story are often more revealing than what they volunteer
- **If they go off-script, follow them** — the most valuable insights come from unexpected tangents
- **Don't try to close the sale** — your only job is to understand their world
- **At minute 18, signal wrap-up** — "I have one or two more questions and then I want to respect your time"

### Post-Interview Checklist (within 2 hours)

- [ ] Stop the recording and save the file
- [ ] Run transcription through LLM to extract themes
- [ ] Write 3 verbatim quotes in the tracker
- [ ] Note the WTP signal
- [ ] Note any red flags or contradictions
- [ ] Send thank-you email within 24 hours
